## Introduction

One of the most frequently cited arguments for the classified [re-entry]({{ 're-entry/' | relative_url }}) vehicle explanation of the Kecksburg incident is simple: [witnesses]({{ 'witnesses/' | relative_url }}) described a metallic object shaped like an acorn, and the General Electric Mark 2 re-entry vehicle was a real Cold War-era object whose profile can look strikingly similar. That resemblance is not imaginary. The Mark 2 was a blunt-bodied re-entry vehicle with a rounded copper nose and a tapered rear section, a form created specifically to survive atmospheric re-entry. ## Could a Mark 2 Look Like the Acorn?

### What the Mark 2 actually looked like

The General Electric Mark 2 was developed during the 1950s as a re-entry vehicle for American ballistic missile programmes. Unlike the long, pointed missile noses that many people imagine, it was based on the then-new “blunt body” concept. Engineers discovered that a rounded front created a detached shock wave that carried much of the heat away from the vehicle during re-entry.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_entry" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Atmospheric entry</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atmospheric entry</span></span></span>

The resulting shape was neither a cylinder nor a sharp cone. It combined a broad rounded front with a narrowing rear section. Smithsonian records describe the vehicle as having a blunt copper nose with the remainder constructed from stainless steel. Surviving examples are roughly five feet in diameter and about five feet high, creating a compact, squat profile rather than a slender rocket shape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://airandspace.si.edu/collection-objects/missile-reentry-vehicle-mark-2/nasm_A19751430000" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: airandspace.si.edu">[National Air and Space Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">airandspace.si.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Air and Space MuseumMissile, Reentry Vehicle, Mark 2Mark 2 reentry vehicle (RV) Made by General Electric, the blunt nose is copp...</span></span></span>

Technical descriptions of the Mark 2 often characterise it as a blunt-body or sphere-cone design. In profile, that form can resemble an acorn, bell, or rounded cone depending on the viewing angle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_entry" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Atmospheric entry</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atmospheric entry</span></span></span>

### Why witnesses saw an acorn

The acorn description became central to Kecksburg lore because multiple later accounts described a metallic object with a rounded upper section and a narrower lower portion. Popular retellings frequently compared it to an oversized acorn or a Volkswagen-sized acorn-shaped craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kecksburg_UFO_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kecksburg UFO incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kecksburg UFO incident</span></span></span>

When advocates of the Mark 2 theory compared witness sketches with museum photographs of surviving Mark 2 vehicles, they noticed several points of overlap:

## Where the Acorn Comparison Fits

The shape argument is strongest when used narrowly. It answers a specific question: was there a real military object in the 1950s and 1960s that looked somewhat like the object described at Kecksburg?

For that limited question, the answer is yes. The Mark 2 provides a plausible visual analogue. Its blunt copper nose, rounded contours and compact dimensions fit the general appearance reported in many later reconstructions of the event. The comparison becomes weaker when stretched beyond appearance. Witness reports varied significantly. Some descriptions were recorded years after the incident. ## Why Shape Evidence Is Not Identification

The major limitation of the Mark 2 theory is that many different objects can share a similar outline. Engineers repeatedly arrived at blunt, rounded shapes because the physics of atmospheric re-entry favoured them. The Mark 2 was not the only re-entry vehicle to use this approach, even if it was one of the earliest and most recognisable examples.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_entry" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Atmospheric entry</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Atmospheric entry</span></span></span>
